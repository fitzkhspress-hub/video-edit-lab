Edit a MOTS Package in Adobe Express

Turn your man-on-the-street answers into a tight, even, well-ordered package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the MOTS Rubric
Start
Before you open Express

Have these ready

A MOTS lives or dies in the field. Don't open Adobe Express until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't judge audio through laptop speakers.
Step 1 of 8

Start a blank video project

  1. On the Adobe Express home screen, select Video.
  2. Hover over the Video project option and click the card to open a blank project. Skip Browse templates. Templates add effects and title cards, and the rubric doesn't allow them.
  3. Check the size: widescreen 16:9 (1920 × 1080) to match the broadcast.
  4. Rename the project with your slug, like MOTS-lunch-question, so your producer can find it.
The menus are down the left side: Media, Text, Elements. The timeline is across the bottom. Adobe updates Express often, so if a label looks a little different, look for the closest match.
Step 2 of 8

Upload your answers and tag line

  1. Open Media and upload from your device: every answer clip and your tag line clip.
  2. Wait until every upload finishes before you start building.
  3. Watch each clip once. Note who is framed on the left and who is framed on the right. You'll need that in Step 4.
Rubric · Mix of people5 to 7 usable answers. Different grades, 1 or more staff members, more than one background.

Quick check: You watch your clips and only 4 answers are usable. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Trim every answer to 3–8 seconds

  1. Drag one answer onto the timeline.
  2. Trim it: hover over the start or end of the clip, then drag the edge. The timeline shows the length in seconds.
  3. Start on the first real word. Cut "um," "so," "like" and laughs before the answer.
  4. To cut out a restart in the middle, split on both sides of it and delete the middle piece. To split, put the playhead on the spot and press S, right-click and choose Split, or use the clip's ⋯ menu → Split.
  5. Repeat for every answer. Keep each one at 3 to 8 seconds.
Rubric · Trims + cutsEvery answer is 3 to 8 seconds and starts on the first real word. Hard cuts only. No effects or title cards.

Quick check: An answer goes: "Um, so, like… I think the pizza is actually good?" Where should the clip start?

Step 4 of 8

Put the answers in order

  1. Open strong. Put a clear, interesting answer first to hook the viewer.
  2. Save your best answer for last.
  3. Alternate left and right. Put someone framed on the left next to someone framed on the right, so the faces seem to answer each other.
  4. Drag the tag line clip to the very end. It's the last thing on the timeline.
  5. Clips sit right next to each other: hard cuts only. Don't use the + → Add transition button, and don't add music, effects or a title card with the question. The anchor's lead-in asks the question.
Rubric · Order + lengthStrong open, best answer last, alternates left/right. 30 to 90 seconds. Ends on the show tag.
If everyone ended up framed on the same side, alternate as well as you can and fix it on your next shoot.
Step 5 of 8

Make every voice equally loud

  1. Play the package with headphones. Listen for any answer that's louder or softer than the rest.
  2. Click that clip to open its settings, then find Volume.
  3. Raise a soft clip or lower a loud one until it matches the others.
  4. Play it again from the start. A viewer should never need to touch the volume.
Rubric · AudioEvery answer is clear. Every voice is equally loud. No need to touch the volume.
Volume can't fix an answer buried in hallway noise or wind. If you can't understand the words, cut that answer or reshoot it.

Quick check: Answer 3 is much quieter than the others. Best fix?

Step 6 of 8

Add a lower third to every person

  1. Move the playhead to the start of the first answer.
  2. Open Text, add a text box and type the person's name on one line and their grade or title on the next.
  3. Drag it to the lower third of the frame, with nothing covering the face. Use a plain, readable style.
  4. On the timeline, drag the text layer's edges so it shows during that answer and ends before the cut to the next person. If you can't see the text layer, turn on Show layer timing.
  5. For the next person, copy and paste the text box (Ctrl/⌘ C, then Ctrl/⌘ V) so every lower third matches. Then change the words and move it under that answer.
  6. Check every spelling against your notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Names + lower thirdsEvery person has a lower third with name and grade or title, spelled the way they said it.

Quick check: Which lower third earns full points?

Step 7 of 8

Check the run time and watch it all the way through

  1. Look at the total length on the timeline. It should be 30 to 90 seconds.
  2. If you're over, cut your weakest answer or tighten trims, but keep every answer at 3 seconds or more. If you're under, add another usable answer.
  3. Look for black frames or blank gaps between clips, especially at the start and end.
  4. Watch it once all the way through with the rubric next to you. Ask yourself: would anyone in this video be embarrassed? If so, cut it.
Rubric · Order + length30 to 90 seconds. Over 90 seconds drops this row to a 2.

Quick check: Your package runs 1:42 with 7 answers. What do you do?

Step 8 of 8

Download and turn it in

  1. Click Download in the top right.
  2. Choose MP4.
  3. Choose 1080p. Skip 4K: the file is bigger and harder to share.
  4. Watch the downloaded file once to make sure the audio and every lower third came through.
  5. Turn it in with your question, run time and lead-in.
Rubric · Deadlines + respect.mp4 in Friday. Final with fixes by Tuesday, end of class. Permission asked. Nothing embarrasses anyone.
Lost the download? Click the Your stuff folder in the left bar, choose Files, open the video's ⋯ menu and select Download.
