Edit a MOTS Package in Premiere

Turn your man-on-the-street answers into a tight, even, well-ordered package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the MOTS Rubric
Start
Before you open Premiere

Have these ready

A MOTS lives or dies in the field. Don't open Premiere until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't judge audio through laptop speakers.
Premiere Pro or Premiere? In version 26 (January 2026), Adobe renamed Premiere Pro to just Adobe Premiere. It's the same app. These steps match the current version. Need a tour of the panels? See Step 2 of the Premiere News Package module.
Step 1 of 8

Create the project and import your clips

  1. On the home screen, select New Project.
  2. Name the project with your slug, like MOTS-lunch-question. For the location, choose your story folder.
  3. In the import screen, go to your story folder. Select an answer clip first, then the rest of your answers and your tag line clip. Your picks collect in the Selection Tray at the bottom.
  4. Under Import Settings, turn on Create new sequence, then select Create.
  5. Premiere drops your clips onto the timeline. You'll rebuild it in order, so select everything in the timeline and press Delete. Your clips stay safe in the Project panel.
  6. Save with Ctrl+S (Windows) or ⌘+S (Mac), and save often.
The first clip you select sets the sequence's frame size and frame rate. Starting with one of your camera clips keeps the settings right.
Step 2 of 8

Watch and label your answers

  1. In the Project panel, double-click an answer. It opens in the Source Monitor. Press Space to play it.
  2. Note whether the person is framed on the left or framed on the right. You'll need that in Step 4.
  3. Rename the clip so you can find it fast: click its name in the Project panel and type the person's last name plus L or R, like Ortiz-R.
  4. Skip any answer that's shaky, out of focus or hard to hear. It isn't usable.
Rubric · Mix of people5 to 7 usable answers. Different grades, 1 or more staff members, more than one background.

Quick check: You watch your clips and only 4 answers are usable. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Trim every answer to 3–8 seconds

  1. Open an answer in the Source Monitor. Play to the first real word and press I to set the In point. Leave out "um," "so," "like" and laughs.
  2. Play to the end of the last word and press O to set the Out point. The marked length shows in the lower right of the Source Monitor. Keep it at 3 to 8 seconds.
  3. Drag the picture from the Source Monitor onto V1 of the timeline. Its audio lands on A1. Place each new answer right after the last one, with no gap.
  4. To cut out a restart in the middle, move the playhead to the start of it and press Ctrl+K (Windows) or ⌘+K (Mac). Do the same at the end of it. Click the middle piece and press Shift+Delete to remove it and close the gap.
  5. Repeat for every answer.
Rubric · Trims + cutsEvery answer is 3 to 8 seconds and starts on the first real word. Hard cuts only. No effects or title cards.

Quick check: An answer goes: "Um, so, like… I think the pizza is actually good?" Where should you set the In point?

Step 4 of 8

Put the answers in order

  1. Open strong. Put a clear, interesting answer first to hook the viewer.
  2. Save your best answer for last.
  3. Alternate left and right. Put someone framed on the left next to someone framed on the right, so the faces seem to answer each other. Your L and R labels from Step 2 make this quick.
  4. To move an answer, hold Ctrl+Alt (Windows) or ⌘+Option (Mac) and drag it to its new spot. The other clips slide over, and no gap is left.
  5. Drag the tag line clip to the very end. It's the last thing on the timeline.
  6. Clips sit right next to each other: hard cuts only. Don't add transitions (skip Ctrl+D and Shift+D), music, effects, or a title card with the question. The anchor's lead-in asks the question.
Rubric · Order + lengthStrong open, best answer last, alternates left/right. 30 to 90 seconds. Ends on the show tag.
If everyone ended up framed on the same side, alternate as well as you can and fix it on your next shoot.
Step 5 of 8

Make every voice equally loud

  1. Find the Audio Meters next to the timeline. If you can't see them, choose Window → Audio Meters.
  2. Play the package with headphones and watch the meters. Every voice should peak in about the same spot, between −12 and −6 dB.
  3. Select a clip that's louder or softer than the rest. In the Properties panel, drag the Volume slider left (quieter) or right (louder) until it matches the others.
  4. Play it again from the start. A viewer should never need to touch the volume.
Rubric · AudioEvery answer is clear. Every voice is equally loud. No need to touch the volume.
Volume can't fix an answer buried in hallway noise or wind. If you can't understand the words, cut that answer or reshoot it.

Quick check: Answer 3 is much quieter than the others. Best fix?

Step 6 of 8

Add a lower third to every person

  1. Move the playhead to the start of the first answer. Click an empty spot on the timeline so no clip is selected.
  2. Choose the Type tool (T) and click in the lower part of the Program Monitor. Type the person's name, press Enter (Windows) or Return (Mac), and type their grade or title. Premiere puts the text on its own clip on V2.
  3. Right-click the text and choose Edit Properties. In the Properties panel, pick a plain, readable font and size. Press V and drag the text into the lower third, with nothing covering the face.
  4. On the timeline, drag the text clip's edges so it shows during that answer and ends before the cut to the next person.
  5. For the next person, hold Alt (Windows) or Option (Mac) and drag the text clip over the next answer. That makes a copy, so every lower third matches. With the Type tool, double-click the copy in the Program Monitor and change the words.
  6. Check every spelling against your notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Names + lower thirdsEvery person has a lower third with name and grade or title, spelled the way they said it.

Quick check: Which lower third earns full points?

Step 7 of 8

Check the run time and watch it all the way through

  1. Click the timeline, press Home to jump to the start, then press ↓ (Down Arrow) until you reach the end of the tag line. The playhead's timecode is your run time. It should be 30 to 90 seconds.
  2. If you're over, cut your weakest answer or tighten trims, but keep every answer at 3 seconds or more. If you're under, add another usable answer.
  3. Look for empty gaps and black frames, especially at the start and end. Right-click a gap and choose Ripple Delete to close it.
  4. Press Home and watch it once all the way through with the rubric next to you. Ask yourself: would anyone in this video be embarrassed? If so, cut it.
Rubric · Order + length30 to 90 seconds. Over 90 seconds drops this row to a 2.
Timecode reads hours:minutes:seconds:frames. So 00:01:30:00 is exactly 90 seconds.

Quick check: Your package runs 00:01:42:00 with 7 answers. What do you do?

Step 8 of 8

Export and turn it in

  1. Click the timeline so your sequence is selected. Choose File → Export → Media, or press Ctrl+M (Windows) or ⌘+M (Mac).
  2. In the left column, choose Media File. Set the file name to your slug and the location to your story folder.
  3. Set Format to H.264 and Preset to Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate, then select Export.
  4. Watch the exported file once to make sure the audio and every lower third came through.
  5. Turn it in with your question, run time and lead-in.
Rubric · Deadlines + respect.mp4 in Friday. Final with fixes by Tuesday, end of class. Permission asked. Nothing embarrasses anyone.
Turn in the exported .mp4, not the project file. Your producer can't play a project file.
