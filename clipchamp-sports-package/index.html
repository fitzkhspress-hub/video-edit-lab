Edit a Sports Package in Clipchamp

Turn your game footage, VO, sound bites and tag into a finished sports package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the Sports Package Rubric
Start
Before you open Clipchamp

Have these ready

A sports package is built from what happened on the field. Don't open Clipchamp until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. Crowd noise and whistles can bury a voice, and you won't hear it through laptop speakers.

Shortcuts you'll use: S split at the playhead · Space play/pause · Ctrl/Cmd + Z undo · Ctrl/Cmd + = / - zoom the timeline

Step 1 of 8

Start a blank project

  1. Open Clipchamp and select Create a new video. Skip the templates. Sports templates add flashy effects and title cards. Your package should look like news, not a hype reel.
  2. Click the project name at the top (it starts as Untitled video) and rename it with your slug, like SPORTS-volleyball-district, so your producer can find it.
  3. Leave the frame at widescreen 16:9 to match the broadcast.
Clipchamp updates its layout often. If a button has moved, look nearby for the same name.
Step 2 of 8

Import your own media

  1. Click Import media and add your VO, your tag, your sound bite clips and your game footage. They show up in Your media.
  2. Game clips are often long. Watch each one once and write down the time of every play your script names, so you can find it fast in Step 4.
  3. Wait until every file finishes importing before you start building.
Rubric · B-rollWide, medium and tight sequences. Steady, in focus, real action. A staged shot costs you a point.

Quick check: You missed the game-winning play on camera. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: VO, bites and tag

Build the package around the audio. The words decide the length, and the game footage goes on top.

  1. Drag your VO from Your media onto the timeline and line it up with 0:00.
  2. No VO file? Select Record & create, then Audio. Allow the microphone, click the red mic button, read your script, then click Stop. Choose Retake recording if you stumbled, or Save and edit to keep it.
  3. Find each spot where a bite plays. Click the VO, park the playhead there and press S to split it. Drag the second half right to open a space.
  4. Drag the sound bite clip onto the video track in that space. Trim it: park the playhead where the thought starts, press S and delete the extra. Do the same right after the out-cue.
  5. Put your tag at the very end. Your package ends on it.
Rubric · Sound biteOne complete thought, 8 to 12 seconds. A feeling or opinion. Clean out-cue.
Zoom in on the timeline (Ctrl/Cmd + =) to split exactly between words.

Quick check: Which part of the player's interview makes the best bite?

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with game footage

  1. Under each line of VO, drag in the play that line describes. Show what the words say. If the VO names a player's serve, show that player serving.
  2. Cut long game clips down to the action with S. Start just before the play, cut once it's over and delete the rest.
  3. Build sequences: a wide of the play, a medium of the player, a tight of the ball, hands or face.
  4. Use reaction shots (bench, crowd, coach) to show how the moment felt.
  5. Use straight cuts only. No transitions, no slow motion or speed changes, no music.
Rubric · EditPictures match words. Straight cuts. A mismatch or a repeated shot costs you a point.

Quick check: Your VO says, "Junior Jayden Brooks broke free for a 40-yard touchdown." What goes under that line?

Step 5 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Click a game clip, then open the Audio tab in the property panel on the right.
  2. Drag the volume slider left until the nat sound (crowd, whistles, the ball, the band) sits under your VO. Do this for every game clip.
  3. Never drag nat sound to 0, and don't detach and delete it. The rubric wants nat sound under every VO.
  4. Click the VO, each bite and the tag and adjust them so the voices sound equally loud. The slider goes up to 200%.
  5. Bites recorded at the game may have crowd noise behind them. If the speaker is hard to hear, turn the bite up, or pick a cleaner bite.
  6. Play the whole package with headphones. Crackle or fuzz means a clip is too loud, so lower it.
Rubric · AudioVoices clear at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound under every VO.
Clipchamp's volume slider shows a percentage, not decibels. Set levels by ear in Clipchamp, then check the −12 to −6 dB peak the way your class does.

Quick check: The crowd is so loud it drowns out your VO. Best fix?

Step 6 of 8

Add the lower thirds

  1. Open the Text tab in the left toolbar and choose a plain, readable style. Skip anything marked with a diamond. That's a premium feature, and it can block your export.
  2. Drag the text onto the timeline on a track above the sound bite.
  3. Start it 1 second after the bite starts. Drag its trim handles so it holds for 4 seconds.
  4. With the text selected, open the Text tab in the property panel. Type the speaker's name and title, like Head Volleyball Coach or Senior Outside Hitter.
  5. In the preview, drag the text to the lower part of the frame, clear of the speaker's face. Repeat for every speaker, then check each spelling against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Lower thirdLower third correct: spelled right and timed right. In Cougar News, it starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.
Putting the final score or a stat on screen? Check it against the source one more time. Every name, stat and number is checked before the final.

Quick check: The online roster spells your player's name "Kaitlynn," but your notes say "Caitlin." What do you do?

Step 7 of 8

Clean up and check the run time

  1. Look for gaps on the timeline. Hover over a gap and click Delete this gap, or right-click a tiny gap and choose it there.
  2. Remove any transitions, music or repeated shots you added by accident.
  3. Check the total length. It should be within 2 seconds of your target. If you're over, tighten VO pauses or trim plays down to the action. If you're under, ask your producer before you add anything.
  4. Watch it once all the way through with the rubric next to you. Make sure the package ends on your tag.
Rubric · TimingWithin 2 seconds of the target (:30 · :45 · :60). Read out loud and timed.

Quick check: Your target is :60 and your package runs :53. Where does that land in the Timing row?

Step 8 of 8

Export and turn it in

  1. Remove anything marked with a diamond (premium) so the export isn't blocked.
  2. Click Export in the top-right corner of the editor.
  3. Choose 1080p and wait for the progress bar to finish. The MP4 saves to your computer, usually in Downloads.
  4. Watch the exported file once to make sure the audio and lower thirds came through.
  5. Turn in everything the full turn-in asks for. A missing step costs you a point.
Rubric · Deadlines + respectFinal by Tuesday, end of class. Full turn-in. Nothing embarrasses anyone, including the other team.
If Clipchamp asks about gaps when you click Export, choose to remove them. Lost the file? Open the project again from your Clipchamp home page and export it again.
