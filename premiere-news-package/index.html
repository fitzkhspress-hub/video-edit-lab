Edit a News Package in Premiere Pro

Turn your script, VO, sound bite and b-roll into a finished news package, one step at a time.

About 13 minutes10 steps + final checkBuilt on the News Story Rubric
Start
Before you open Premiere

Have these ready

Editing goes fast when the reporting is done. Don't open Premiere until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't judge audio through laptop speakers.
Premiere Pro or Premiere? In version 26 (January 2026), Adobe renamed Premiere Pro to just Adobe Premiere. It's the same app. These steps match the current version. Older versions may look a little different.
Step 1 of 10

Create the project and import your media

  1. On the home screen, select New Project.
  2. Name the project with your story's slug. For the location, choose your story folder so the project and clips stay together.
  3. In the import screen, go to your story folder and select your clips. Click a b-roll video clip first, then add your VO, your sound bite clip and the rest of your b-roll. Your picks collect in the Selection Tray at the bottom.
  4. Under Import Settings, turn on Create new sequence.
  5. Select Create. Premiere opens in Edit mode with your new sequence.
  6. Save now with Ctrl+S (Windows) or ⌘+S (Mac), and save often.
Why click a video clip first? Premiere sets the sequence's frame size and frame rate from the first clip you select. If you start with your VO (audio only), your sequence settings can come out wrong.
Creating a sequence this way also drops your clips onto the timeline. You'll rebuild the timeline in order in the next steps, so select everything in the timeline and press Delete to clear it. Your clips stay safe in the Project panel.

Step 3 of 10

Build the spine: lay down your VO

Build the package around the audio. The words decide the length, and the pictures go on top.

  1. Drag your VO from the Project panel onto audio track A1, starting at the very beginning of the timeline.
  2. Play it back with the Space bar. Listen for flubs, coughs and long pauses.
  3. To cut out a flub, move the playhead to the start of it and press Ctrl+K (Windows) or ⌘+K (Mac) to add a cut. Do the same at the end of it. You can also use the Razor tool (C) and click the clip. Press V to go back to the Selection tool.
  4. Click the piece you cut out and press Shift+Delete. That's a ripple delete: it removes the piece and slides everything after it over, so no gap is left.
  5. Wherever your script calls for the sound bite, leave the VO split so there's room for the bite. (Cut the VO there and drag the second part to the right.)
Plain Delete leaves an empty gap behind. Shift+Delete closes the gap.
Step 4 of 10

Mark and place the sound bite

  1. Double-click your sound bite clip in the Project panel. It opens in the Source Monitor.
  2. Play to the first word of the bite and press I to set the In point.
  3. Play to the end of the out-cue and press O to set the Out point. The duration of your marked section shows in the lower right of the Source Monitor.
  4. Drag the picture from the Source Monitor into the space you left in the timeline, on V1. Its audio lands on A1, in line with your VO.
  5. Make sure there's no gap or overlap where the VO meets the bite.
Rubric · Sound biteOne complete thought, 8 to 12 seconds. A feeling or opinion, not a fact. Ends on a clean out-cue.

Quick check: Your marked bite runs 15 seconds. What do you do?

Step 5 of 10

Cover the VO with b-roll

  1. Open a b-roll clip in the Source Monitor. Mark the best 3 to 4 seconds with I and O. Pick the part that's steady and in focus.
  2. Drag it onto V2, above the VO it matches. Its nat sound lands on A2, so it stays separate from your voices on A1. (If the audio lands on a different track, drag it down to A2.)
  3. Show what the words say. When the VO names something, the viewer should be seeing it.
  4. Build sequences: a wide, then a medium, then a tight of the same action.
  5. Keep V2 empty over the sound bite so the speaker shows through from V1.
  6. Use straight cuts only. No transitions, no music, and don't use the same shot twice.
Rubric · B-roll + EditWide, medium and tight sequences of real action, never staged. Pictures match words. Shots stay up 3 to 4 seconds. Straight cuts, no music, no repeats.
Skip the shortcuts that aren't your reporting. Don't fill gaps with Adobe Stock footage, and don't use Generative Extend to stretch a short shot. It adds frames made by AI that no camera recorded. If your b-roll is thin, shoot more.

Quick check: Your last wide shot is 2 seconds too short to cover the VO. Best fix?

Step 6 of 10

Mix the audio

  1. Find the Audio Meters next to the timeline. If you can't see them, choose Window → Audio Meters.
  2. Play the package and watch the meters. Voices should peak between −12 and −6 dB.
  3. To change a clip's level, select it on the timeline. In the Properties panel, drag the Volume slider left (quieter) or right (louder).
  4. Match your VO and bite so the loudness doesn't jump when you switch between them.
  5. Select the nat sound clips on A2 and lower them until they sit under your VO. You can select several clips at once and lower them together. Don't use Mute, and don't turn them all the way down.
  6. Listen through once more with headphones and fix any spot that's too loud or too soft.
Rubric · AudioVoices are clear and peak at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound plays under the b-roll, never muted.

Quick check: While your VO plays, the meters keep hitting −2 dB. What do you do?

Step 7 of 10

Add the lower third

  1. Move the playhead to 1 second after your sound bite starts. The playhead's time shows at the left under the Program Monitor.
  2. Click an empty spot on the timeline so no clip is selected. Choose the Type tool (T) and click in the lower part of the Program Monitor.
  3. Type the speaker's name. Press Enter (Windows) or Return (Mac) and type their title on the next line. Premiere puts the text on its own clip above your video.
  4. Right-click the text in the Program Monitor and choose Edit Properties. In the Properties panel, pick a plain, readable font and size. Turn on a background or shadow if the text is hard to read over the video.
  5. Press V and drag the text into the lower third of the frame, with nothing covering the speaker's face. To see the safe area, click the wrench under the Program Monitor and turn on Safe Margins.
  6. Set the length: right-click the text clip on the timeline, choose Speed/Duration, type 00:00:04:00 and click OK.
  7. Check the spelling against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Lower thirdSpelled right, starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.
Timecode reads hours:minutes:seconds:frames. So 00:00:04:00 is exactly 4 seconds.

Quick check: Your bite starts at 00:00:14:00. When should the lower third appear and disappear?

Step 8 of 10

Check the run time and watch it all the way through

  1. Click the timeline, press Home to jump to the start, then press ↓ (Down Arrow) to jump from cut to cut until you reach the end of the last clip. The playhead's timecode is your run time.
  2. It should be within 2 seconds of your target. If you're over, tighten VO pauses or trim shots, but keep every shot at 3 seconds or more. If you're under, ask your producer before you add anything.
  3. Look for empty gaps and black frames, especially at the very start and end. Right-click a gap and choose Ripple Delete to close it.
  4. Make sure no b-roll or text hangs past the end of your audio.
  5. Press Home and watch it once all the way through, start to finish, with the rubric next to you. One more careful watch is often worth points.
Rubric · TimingWithin 2 seconds of the target length (:30 · :45 · :60).

Quick check: Your target is :45 and your run time reads 00:00:49:00. What does that cost you?

Step 9 of 10

Export and turn it in

  1. Click the timeline so your sequence is selected.
  2. Choose File → Export → Media, or press Ctrl+M (Windows) or ⌘+M (Mac).
  3. In the left column, choose Media File. Skip the social media destinations.
  4. Set the file name to your slug and the location to your story folder.
  5. Set Format to H.264 and Preset to Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate. That matches your sequence's frame size and frame rate.
  6. Select Export and wait for it to finish.
  7. Open the exported file and watch it once to make sure the audio and lower third came through.
  8. Turn it in with everything listed in the Deadlines row of your rubric.
Rubric · DeadlinesDrafts on time Tuesday. Final by Wednesday, 7:30 a.m. A late final is a zero, and you'll have to redo it.
Turn in the exported .mp4, not the project file. Your producer can't play a project file.
