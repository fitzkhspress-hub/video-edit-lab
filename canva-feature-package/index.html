Edit a Feature Package in Canva

Turn your interviews, VO, b-roll and nat sound into a finished feature package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the Feature Rubric
Start
Before you open Canva

Have these ready

A feature package lives or dies on its people, pictures and sound. Don't open Canva until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't hear room echo or a weak nat sound through laptop speakers.
Step 1 of 8

Start a blank video project

  1. On the Canva home screen, select Video. If you don't see it, select Create, then Videos.
  2. Choose a blank video design. Skip the templates. They add title cards, animations and effects. Your package should look like news.
  3. Check the size: widescreen 16:9 (1920 × 1080) to match the broadcast.
  4. Rename the project with your slug, like FEATURE-robotics-team, so your producer can find it.
The panels you'll use are down the left side, including Uploads and Text. The timeline is across the bottom. If you see page thumbnails instead of a timeline, select the pencil icon on a page to switch to the Video Timeline. Canva updates often, so if a label looks a little different, look for the closest match.
Step 2 of 8

Upload, then pick your best shots

  1. Open Uploads and select Upload files. Upload from your device: your VO, your interview clips and your b-roll.
  2. Wait until every upload finishes processing before you start building. You'll need that for J and L cuts in Step 5.
  3. Watch your b-roll once. Set aside any shot that is shaky, blurry, too dark, too bright or the wrong color. Those cost points in Video Production, even if they're short.
  4. Find your best shot, the one with the strongest picture and nat sound. You'll open with it in Step 3.
Rubric · Video productionRule of thirds with head room and nose room. Every shot well lit, steady and in focus. Clear sequences, including WALLDO. Stock video from Canva's library isn't your reporting, so don't use it.

Quick check: Your only shot of the robot's first run is a little shaky. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: open, VO and bites

Build the package around the audio. The words and nat sound set the story's shape, and the b-roll goes on top.

  1. Open with your best b-roll and let the nat sound pop, 1 to 3 seconds of real sound (a cheer, a buzzer, a laugh) before anyone talks.
  2. Drag your VO onto the timeline after the open.
  3. Wherever your script calls for a bite, drag that interview clip in. Use bites from at least 2 credible sources.
  4. Trim a clip: select it, then drag its left or right handle. For exact cuts, zoom the timeline by holding Ctrl (Windows) or ⌘ (Mac) and scrolling.
  5. To split a clip, select it and put the playhead where you want the cut. Then press S, or use the ••• (More) menu → Split.
  6. To remove a section, split on both sides of it and delete the middle piece. Use this to cut gaps or flubs out of your VO.
  7. Check your gold coins: a strong bite or nat sound pop at the start, in the middle and at the end.
Rubric · Story structure + writingClear beginning, middle and end, with gold coins. The VO sounds like talking and doesn't repeat what the bites say.

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with b-roll

  1. Under each line of VO, drag in the b-roll that shows that line. Say dog, see dog. If the VO names a student soldering a wire, show that student soldering.
  2. Build sequences: wide, then medium, then tight. Mix in WALLDO shots to keep it moving.
  3. Use each shot only once.
  4. Don't put two shots of the same person from the same angle back to back. That's a jump cut. Put a different shot between them.
  5. Use straight cuts. When you hover between two clips, Canva offers Add transition. Skip it, and don't add animations or effects.
Rubric · Visual storytellingStart with the best b-roll. Nat sound pops. Say dog, see dog. A shot used twice drops you to a 2.

Quick check: Your VO says, "Senior Maya Ortiz has rebuilt the arm three times." What goes under that line?

Step 5 of 8

Make J and L cuts

A J cut lets you hear the next clip before you see it. An L cut lets the sound keep going after the picture changes. Both make your edit flow, and the rubric needs them for a 4 in Sequences & Transitions.

  1. Select an interview or b-roll clip in the editor. Right-click it and choose Extract audio, or open Audio tools and select Extract. The sound shows up on the timeline as its own audio item, and now you can move it apart from the picture.
  2. Play that spot back. If you hear the sound twice, like an echo, set the video clip's volume to 0. The sound now lives on the audio item, so you aren't muting your nat sound.
  3. L cut: drag the end handle of the interview's video to the left so your b-roll comes in sooner. Leave the interview's audio item long. The speaker keeps talking while we see what they're talking about.
  4. J cut: drag the next clip's audio item a second or two to the left, so it starts under the shot before. We hear the crowd, the band or the speaker just before we see them.
  5. Play each cut back. The sound should flow under the picture change without a gap or a jump.
Rubric · Sequences & transitionsClean cuts. J and L cuts. No jump cuts or flash frames. With no J or L cuts, the best you can earn is a 3.
Don't see Extract audio? It only works on video you uploaded yourself, after it finishes processing. Select the clip directly in the editor and try again.

Quick check: You hear the marching band's drums for 2 seconds, then the picture cuts to the band. What kind of cut is that?

Step 6 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Select a clip or audio item, then select the volume icon in the toolbar above the editor. Use the slider or type a number.
  2. Set your VO and interviews so the voices are clear and even. When you switch from VO to a bite, the loudness shouldn't jump.
  3. Turn nat sound down so it plays under your VO. Never set it to zero.
  4. Between VO lines, give the nat sound a moment on its own so it pops: the buzzer, the laugh, the cheer. If it's too quiet there, split that piece and turn just that piece up.
  5. Skip Balance all. It can push the nat sound up to the same level as the voices.
  6. Play the whole package with headphones. Fix any spot that's too loud, too soft or hard to understand.
Rubric · Audio productionVoices at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound clear and in the right places. VO clear the whole way through.
Canva's volume slider isn't a dB meter. Set levels by ear, then check the peaks the way your class checks audio before you turn in.

Quick check: Your VO is easy to hear, but you muted all the b-roll to get it that way. What happens on the rubric?

Step 7 of 8

Add lower thirds and fact graphics

  1. Move the playhead to 1 second after an interview starts.
  2. Open Text, add a text box and type the speaker's name on one line. On the next line, type their title, like Robotics Team Captain or Engineering Teacher.
  3. Drag it to the lower third of the frame, with nothing covering the speaker's face. Use a plain, readable style.
  4. On the timeline, drag the text layer's edges so it holds for 4 seconds. Text sits on its own track above the video. If you see page thumbnails instead of a timeline, switch to the Video Timeline.
  5. Repeat for every speaker. If a key fact needs to be on screen, like a date, place or number, add a graphic for it the same way.
  6. Check each spelling and number against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · GraphicsCorrect graphics for every interview and any other facts. One misspelling drops you to a 2. In Cougar News, a lower third starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.

Quick check: The club's website spells the captain's name "Jaxon," but your notes say "Jackson." What do you do?

Step 8 of 8

Watch it through, download and turn it in

  1. Watch the whole package once with the rubric next to you. Look for flash frames (a single stray frame), black gaps and jump cuts, especially at the start and end.
  2. Listen for every name. Is each one said the way that person says it?
  3. Click Share in the top right, then Download. Choose MP4 Video and 1080p. Skip 4K: the file is bigger and harder to share.
  4. Watch the downloaded file once to make sure the audio, J and L cuts and graphics came through.
  5. Turn in your draft on Friday. Fix what your producer flags, then turn in your final by Tuesday, end of class, with every turn-in step complete.
Rubric · Deadlines + respectDraft on Friday and final by Tuesday, end of class. Full turn-in. Nothing embarrasses anyone.
