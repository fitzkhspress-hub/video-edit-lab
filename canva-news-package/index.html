Edit a News Package in Canva

Turn your script, VO, sound bite and b-roll into a finished news package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the News Story Rubric
Start
Before you open Canva

Have these ready

Editing goes fast when the reporting is done. Don't open Canva until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't judge audio through laptop speakers.
Step 1 of 8

Start a blank video project

  1. On the Canva home screen, select Video. If you don't see it, select Create, then Videos.
  2. Choose a blank video design. Skip the templates. Your package should look like news, not a promo.
  3. Check the size: it should be widescreen 16:9 (1920 × 1080) to match the broadcast.
  4. Rename the project with your story's slug so your producer can find it.
The panels you'll use are down the left side, including Uploads and Text. The timeline is across the bottom. If you see page thumbnails instead of a timeline, select the pencil icon on a page to switch to the Video Timeline. Canva updates often, so if a label looks a little different, look for the closest match.
Step 2 of 8

Upload your own media

  1. Open Uploads and select Upload files. Upload from your device: your VO, your sound bite clip and your b-roll clips.
  2. Wait until every upload finishes before you start building.
Rubric · B-rollReal action and faces, never staged. Stock video from Canva's library isn't your reporting, so don't use it.

Quick check: Your b-roll is a little thin. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: VO and sound bite first

Build the package around the audio. The words decide the length, and the pictures go on top.

  1. Drag your VO onto the timeline.
  2. Wherever your script calls for the bite, drag the sound bite clip onto the timeline.
  3. Trim the bite: select the clip, then drag its left or right handle. For exact cuts, zoom the timeline by holding Ctrl (Windows) or ⌘ (Mac) and scrolling.
  4. To split a clip, select it and put the playhead where you want the cut. Then press S, or use the ••• (More) menu → Split.
  5. To remove a section, split on both sides of it and delete the middle piece. Use this to cut gaps or flubs out of your VO.
Rubric · Sound biteOne complete thought, 8 to 12 seconds, ending on a clean out-cue. Don't cut it off mid-thought.

Quick check: Your bite runs 15 seconds. What do you do?

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with b-roll

  1. Drag b-roll onto the timeline under each line of VO. Show what the words say.
  2. Build sequences: a wide, then a medium, then a tight of the same action.
  3. Trim each shot so it stays up 3 to 4 seconds.
  4. Use straight cuts only. When you hover between two clips, Canva offers Add transition. Skip it.
  5. No music from Canva's audio library, and don't use the same shot twice.
Rubric · EditPictures match words. Shots stay up 3 to 4 seconds. Straight cuts, no music, no repeats.
Step 5 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Select a clip, then select the volume icon in the toolbar above the editor. Use the slider or type a number. Audio tracks and video clips both have it.
  2. Set your VO and sound bite so the voices are clear and even. When you switch from VO to bite, the loudness shouldn't jump.
  3. Turn the b-roll's nat sound down so it plays under your VO. Never set it to zero. Nat sound makes the story feel real.
  4. Skip Balance all. It can push nat sound up to the same level as the voices.
  5. Play the whole package with headphones. Fix any spot that's too loud or too soft.
Rubric · AudioVoices are clear and peak at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound plays under the b-roll, never muted.
Canva's volume slider isn't a dB meter. Get voices to −12 to −6 dB when you record by watching the camera's audio meter. In Canva, set levels by ear, then check the peaks the way your class checks audio before you turn in.

Quick check: The nat sound under your VO is distracting. Best fix?

Step 6 of 8

Add the lower third

  1. Move the playhead to 1 second after your sound bite starts.
  2. Open Text, add a text box and type the speaker's name on one line and their title on the next.
  3. Drag it to the lower third of the frame, with nothing covering the speaker's face. Use a plain, readable style.
  4. On the timeline, drag the text layer's edges so it holds for 4 seconds. Text sits on its own track above the video. If you see page thumbnails instead of a timeline, switch to the Video Timeline.
  5. Check the spelling against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Lower thirdSpelled right, starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.

Quick check: Your bite starts at 0:14. When should the lower third appear and disappear?

Step 7 of 8

Check the run time and watch it all the way through

  1. Look at the package's total length on the timeline. It should be within 2 seconds of your target.
  2. If you're over, tighten VO gaps or shorten shots, but keep each shot at 3 seconds or more. If you're under, ask your producer before you add anything.
  3. Look for any black frames or blank gaps between clips, especially at the very start and end.
  4. Watch it once all the way through, start to finish, with the rubric next to you. One more careful watch is often worth points.
Rubric · TimingWithin 2 seconds of the target length (:30 · :45 · :60).

Quick check: Your target is :45 and your package runs :49. What does that cost you?

Step 8 of 8

Download and turn it in

  1. Click Share in the top right, then Download.
  2. For file type, choose MP4 Video.
  3. For quality, choose 1080p. Skip 4K: the file is bigger and harder to share. Then click Download.
  4. Watch the downloaded file once to make sure the audio and lower third came through.
  5. Turn it in with everything your rubric's turn-in list asks for: lead-in, script, speaker, out-cue and run time.
Rubric · DeadlinesDrafts on time Tuesday. Final by Wednesday, 7:30 a.m. A late final is a zero, and you'll have to redo it.
Lost the download? Canva saves your design automatically. Open it again from Projects on the Canva home screen and download it again.
