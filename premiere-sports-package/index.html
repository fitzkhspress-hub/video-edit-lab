Edit a Sports Package in Premiere

Turn your game footage, VO, sound bites and tag into a finished sports package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the Sports Package Rubric
Start
Before you open Premiere

Have these ready

A sports package is built from what happened on the field. Don't open Premiere until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. Crowd noise and whistles can bury a voice, and you won't hear it through laptop speakers.
Premiere Pro or Premiere? In version 26 (January 2026), Adobe renamed Premiere Pro to just Adobe Premiere. It's the same app. Older versions may look a little different.
Step 1 of 8

Create the project and import your media

  1. On the home screen, select New Project. Name it with your slug, like SPORTS-volleyball-district, and save it in your story folder.
  2. In the import screen, open your story folder. Click a game footage clip first, then add your VO, tag, sound bite clips and the rest of your footage. Your picks collect in the Selection Tray.
  3. Under Import Settings, turn on Create new sequence, then select Create.
  4. Premiere drops your clips onto the timeline. You'll build it in order, so select everything in the timeline and press Delete. Your clips stay safe in the Project panel.
  5. Save with Ctrl+S (Windows) or ⌘+S (Mac), and save often.
Why click a video clip first? Premiere copies the sequence's frame size and frame rate from the first clip you select. Start with an audio file and your settings can come out wrong.
Lost a panel? Open it from the Window menu, or choose Window → Workspaces → Reset to Saved Layout.
Step 2 of 8

Log the plays with markers

Game clips are long. Find your plays once, so you don't scrub through the whole game again in Step 4.

  1. Double-click a game clip in the Project panel. It opens in the Source Monitor.
  2. Play it. Each time you see a play your script names, press M to drop a marker.
  3. Double-click a marker to name it, like Brooks TD or match point.
  4. Mark the reactions too: the bench jumping, the crowd, the coach.
Rubric · B-rollWide, medium and tight sequences. Steady, in focus, real action. A staged shot costs you a point.

Quick check: You missed the game-winning play on camera. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: VO, bites and tag

Build the package around the audio. The words decide the length, and the game footage goes on top.

  1. Drag your VO onto audio track A1, starting at the very beginning of the timeline. Play it with Space.
  2. Cut out flubs: put the playhead at the start of one and press Ctrl+K (Windows) or ⌘+K (Mac). Do the same at the end. Select the piece and press Shift+Delete. That ripple delete closes the gap.
  3. Where your script calls for a bite, cut the VO and drag the second part to the right to make room.
  4. Open the bite in the Source Monitor. Press I on the first word and O after the out-cue. Drag it into the space on V1. Its audio lands on A1, in line with your VO.
  5. Put your tag on A1 at the very end. Your package ends on it.
Rubric · Sound biteOne complete thought, 8 to 12 seconds. A feeling or opinion. Clean out-cue.

Quick check: Which part of the player's interview makes the best bite?

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with game footage

  1. Open a game clip in the Source Monitor and jump to a marker. Set I just before the play and O once it's over.
  2. Drag it onto V2, above the VO line it matches. Its nat sound lands on A2, apart from your voices on A1. (If it lands somewhere else, drag it to A2.)
  3. Show what the words say. If the VO names a player's serve, show that player serving.
  4. Build sequences: a wide of the play, a medium of the player, a tight of the ball, hands or face. Use reaction shots to show how the moment felt.
  5. Keep V2 empty over the bite so the speaker shows through from V1.
  6. Use straight cuts only. No transitions, no slow motion, no music, no repeated shots.
Rubric · EditPictures match words. Straight cuts. A mismatch or a repeated shot costs you a point.
Skip the shortcuts that aren't your reporting. No Adobe Stock footage, and no Generative Extend to stretch a short shot. It adds AI-made frames no camera recorded.

Quick check: Your VO says, "Junior Jayden Brooks broke free for a 40-yard touchdown." What goes under that line?

Step 5 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Find the Audio Meters next to the timeline. If you can't see them, choose Window → Audio Meters.
  2. Play the package. Voices should peak between −12 and −6 dB: VO, bites and tag.
  3. To change a level, select the clip. In the Properties panel, drag the Volume slider left (quieter) or right (louder). Match the VO and bites so the loudness doesn't jump.
  4. Select the nat sound clips on A2 (crowd, whistles, the ball, the band) and lower them together until they sit under your VO. Don't mute them. The rubric wants nat sound under every VO.
  5. A bite recorded at the game may have crowd noise behind it. If the speaker is hard to hear, pick a cleaner bite.
Rubric · AudioVoices clear at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound under every VO.

Quick check: The crowd is so loud it drowns out your VO. Best fix?

Step 6 of 8

Add the lower thirds

  1. Move the playhead to 1 second after a bite starts. Click an empty spot on the timeline so no clip is selected.
  2. Choose the Type tool (T) and click in the lower part of the Program Monitor. Type the speaker's name, press Enter (Windows) or Return (Mac), then type their title, like Head Volleyball Coach or Senior Outside Hitter.
  3. Right-click the text and choose Edit Properties. Pick a plain, readable font. Turn on a background or shadow if it's hard to read over the video.
  4. Press V and drag the text into the lower third, with nothing covering the speaker's face.
  5. Right-click the text clip on the timeline, choose Speed/Duration, type 00:00:04:00 and click OK.
  6. Repeat for every speaker. Check each spelling against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Lower thirdLower third correct: spelled right and timed right. In Cougar News, it starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.
Putting the final score or a stat on screen? Check it against the source one more time. Every name, stat and number is checked before the final.

Quick check: The online roster spells your player's name "Kaitlynn," but your notes say "Caitlin." What do you do?

Step 7 of 8

Check the run time and watch it all the way through

  1. Click the timeline, press Home, then press ↓ to jump cut to cut until the end of the tag. The playhead's timecode is your run time.
  2. It should be within 2 seconds of your target. If you're over, tighten VO pauses or trim plays down to the action. If you're under, ask your producer before you add anything.
  3. Look for gaps and black frames, especially at the start and end. Right-click a gap and choose Ripple Delete.
  4. Make sure no footage or text hangs past the end of your tag.
  5. Press Home and watch it all the way through with the rubric next to you.
Rubric · TimingWithin 2 seconds of the target (:30 · :45 · :60). Read out loud and timed.

Quick check: Your target is :60 and your run time reads 00:00:53:00. Where does that land in the Timing row?

Step 8 of 8

Export and turn it in

  1. Click the timeline, then choose File → Export → Media, or press Ctrl+M (Windows) or ⌘+M (Mac).
  2. In the left column, choose Media File. Skip the social media destinations.
  3. Set the file name to your slug and the location to your story folder.
  4. Set Format to H.264 and Preset to Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate, then select Export.
  5. Watch the exported file once to make sure the audio and lower thirds came through.
  6. Turn in the .mp4 (not the project file) with everything the full turn-in asks for.
Rubric · Deadlines + respectFinal by Tuesday, end of class. Full turn-in. Nothing embarrasses anyone, including the other team.
