Edit a Feature Package in Premiere

Turn your interviews, VO, b-roll and nat sound into a finished feature package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the Feature Rubric
Start
Before you open Premiere

Have these ready

A feature package lives or dies on its people, pictures and sound. Don't open Premiere until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't hear room echo or a weak nat sound through laptop speakers.
Premiere Pro or Premiere? In version 26 (January 2026), Adobe renamed Premiere Pro to just Adobe Premiere. It's the same app. Older versions may look a little different.
Step 1 of 8

Create the project and import your media

  1. On the home screen, select New Project. Name it with your slug, like FEATURE-robotics-team, and save it in your story folder.
  2. In the import screen, open your story folder. Click a b-roll clip first, then add your VO, interview clips and the rest of your b-roll. Your picks collect in the Selection Tray.
  3. Under Import Settings, turn on Create new sequence, then select Create.
  4. Premiere drops your clips onto the timeline. You'll build it in order, so select everything in the timeline and press Delete. Your clips stay safe in the Project panel.
  5. Save with Ctrl+S (Windows) or ⌘+S (Mac), and save often.
Why click a video clip first? Premiere copies the sequence's frame size and frame rate from the first clip you select. Start with an audio file and your settings can come out wrong.
Lost a panel? Open it from the Window menu, or choose Window → Workspaces → Reset to Saved Layout.
Step 2 of 8

Log your best moments, cut your weak shots

Watch everything once now, so you don't scrub through it again later.

  1. Double-click a clip in the Project panel. It opens in the Source Monitor.
  2. Play it. Each time you see a strong moment, press M to drop a marker. Double-click the marker to name it, like arm lifts or team cheers.
  3. Mark your nat sound pops too: a laugh, a buzzer, a motor whining.
  4. Mark your best shot, the one with the strongest picture and nat sound. You'll open with it in Step 3.
  5. Set aside any shot that is shaky, blurry, too dark, too bright or the wrong color. Those cost points in Video Production, even if they're short.
Rubric · Video productionRule of thirds with head room and nose room. Every shot well lit, steady and in focus. Clear sequences, including WALLDO.

Quick check: Your only shot of the robot's first run is a little shaky. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: open, VO and bites

Build the package around the audio. The words and nat sound set the story's shape, and the b-roll goes on top.

  1. Open with your best shot and let the nat sound pop. In the Source Monitor, set I and O around it and drag it to the start of V2. Its sound lands on A2. Give it 1 to 3 seconds before anyone talks.
  2. Drag your VO onto audio track A1, right after the nat pop. Play it with Space.
  3. Cut out flubs: put the playhead at the start of one and press Ctrl+K (Windows) or ⌘+K (Mac). Do the same at the end. Select the piece and press Shift+Delete. That ripple delete closes the gap.
  4. Where your script calls for a bite, cut the VO and drag the second part to the right to make room.
  5. Open the interview in the Source Monitor. Press I on the first word and O after the last. Drag it into the space on V1. Its audio lands on A1, in line with your VO. Use bites from at least 2 credible sources.
  6. Check your gold coins: a strong bite or nat sound pop at the start, in the middle and at the end.
Rubric · Story structure + writingClear beginning, middle and end, with gold coins. The VO sounds like talking and doesn't repeat what the bites say.

Quick check: Which open fits the Feature Rubric best?

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with b-roll

  1. Open a clip in the Source Monitor and jump to a marker. Set I and O, then drag it onto V2, above the VO line it matches. Its nat sound lands on A2, apart from your voices on A1.
  2. Say dog, see dog. If the VO names a student soldering a wire, show that student soldering.
  3. Build sequences: wide, then medium, then tight. Mix in WALLDO shots to keep it moving.
  4. Use each shot only once.
  5. Don't put two shots of the same person from the same angle back to back. That's a jump cut. Put a different shot between them.
  6. Keep V2 empty over most of each bite so the speaker shows through from V1. Use straight cuts: no transitions, no effects.
Rubric · Visual storytellingStart with the best b-roll. Nat sound pops. Say dog, see dog. A shot used twice drops you to a 2.
Skip the shortcuts that aren't your reporting. No Adobe Stock footage, and no Generative Extend to stretch a short shot. It adds AI-made frames no camera recorded.

Quick check: Your VO says, "Senior Maya Ortiz has rebuilt the arm three times." What goes under that line?

Step 5 of 8

Make J and L cuts

A J cut lets you hear the next clip before you see it. An L cut lets the sound keep going after the picture changes. Both make your edit flow, and the rubric needs them for a 4 in Sequences & Transitions.

The easy way: use V2. Your b-roll sits on its own track, so you can make both cuts just by sliding b-roll over the edge of a bite.

  1. L cut: drag the start of the b-roll that follows a bite to the left on V2, or add a new b-roll clip there, so it covers the last 2 to 3 seconds of a bite. We see what the speaker is talking about while we still hear them on A1.
  2. J cut: put b-roll on V2 over the first 1 to 2 seconds of a bite. We hear the speaker start talking before we see them.
  3. Keep the lower third on a moment when the speaker is on screen. Slide it later if b-roll now covers its first second.

Two clips side by side on the same track? Use the Rolling Edit tool.

  1. Press N for the Rolling Edit tool.
  2. Hold Alt (Windows) or Option (Mac) and click the cut on the audio track only. Drag left for a J cut or right for an L cut. The picture cut stays put.
  3. Press V to go back to the Selection tool.
Rubric · Sequences & transitionsClean cuts. J and L cuts. No jump cuts or flash frames. With no J or L cuts, the best you can earn is a 3.

Quick check: You hear the marching band's drums for 2 seconds, then the picture cuts to the band. What kind of cut is that?

Step 6 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Find the Audio Meters next to the timeline. If you can't see them, choose Window → Audio Meters.
  2. Play the package. Voices should peak between −12 and −6 dB: VO and every bite.
  3. To change a level, select the clip. In the Properties panel, drag the Volume slider left (quieter) or right (louder). Match the VO and bites so the loudness doesn't jump.
  4. Select the nat sound clips on A2 that sit under VO and lower them together until they play under your voice. Never mute them.
  5. Leave the nat pops (your open, and any moment between VO lines) louder, so they stand out.
  6. If a bite sounds roomy or thin, select it and try Enhance Speech in the Essential Sound panel (Window → Essential Sound, then mark it Dialogue). Listen before and after, and keep whichever sounds more natural.
Rubric · Audio productionVoices at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound clear and in the right places. VO clear the whole way through.

Quick check: Your VO is easy to hear, but you muted A2 to get it that way. What happens on the rubric?

Step 7 of 8

Add lower thirds and fact graphics

  1. Move the playhead to 1 second after a bite starts, on a frame where the speaker is on screen. Click an empty spot on the timeline so no clip is selected.
  2. Choose the Type tool (T) and click in the lower part of the Program Monitor. Type the speaker's name, press Enter (Windows) or Return (Mac), then type their title, like Robotics Team Captain or Engineering Teacher.
  3. Right-click the text and choose Edit Properties. Pick a plain, readable font. Turn on a background or shadow if it's hard to read over the video.
  4. Press V and drag the text into the lower third, with nothing covering the speaker's face.
  5. Right-click the text clip on the timeline, choose Speed/Duration, type 00:00:04:00 and click OK.
  6. Repeat for every speaker. If a key fact needs to be on screen, like a date, place or number, add a graphic for it the same way.
  7. Check each spelling and number against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · GraphicsCorrect graphics for every interview and any other facts. One misspelling drops you to a 2. In Cougar News, a lower third starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.

Quick check: The club's website spells the captain's name "Jaxon," but your notes say "Jackson." What do you do?

Step 8 of 8

Watch it through, export and turn it in

  1. Press Home and watch the whole package with the rubric next to you. Look for flash frames (a single stray frame), black gaps and jump cuts, especially at the start and end.
  2. To find a tiny gap or flash frame, press = to zoom in on the timeline. Right-click a gap and choose Ripple Delete. Press \ to zoom back out to the whole sequence.
  3. Listen for every name. Is each one said the way that person says it?
  4. Click the timeline, then choose File → Export → Media, or press Ctrl+M (Windows) or ⌘+M (Mac). In the left column, choose Media File.
  5. Set the file name to your slug and the location to your story folder. Set Format to H.264 and Preset to Match Source – Adaptive High Bitrate, then select Export.
  6. Watch the exported file once to make sure the audio and graphics came through.
  7. Turn in the .mp4 (not the project file). Draft on Friday. Fix what your producer flags, then turn in your final by Tuesday, end of class, with every turn-in step complete.
Rubric · Deadlines + respectDraft on Friday and final by Tuesday, end of class. Full turn-in. Nothing embarrasses anyone.
