Edit a Sports Package in Canva

Turn your game footage, VO, sound bites and tag into a finished sports package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the Sports Package Rubric
Start
Before you open Canva

Have these ready

A sports package is built from what happened on the field. Don't open Canva until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. Crowd noise and whistles can bury a voice, and you won't hear it through laptop speakers.
Step 1 of 8

Start a blank video project

  1. On the Canva home screen, select Video. If you don't see it, select Create, then Videos.
  2. Choose a blank video design. Skip the templates. Sports templates add flashy effects and title cards. Your package should look like news, not a hype reel.
  3. Check the size: widescreen 16:9 (1920 × 1080) to match the broadcast.
  4. Rename the project with your slug, like SPORTS-volleyball-district, so your producer can find it.
The panels you'll use are down the left side, including Uploads and Text. The timeline is across the bottom. If you see page thumbnails instead of a timeline, select the pencil icon on a page to switch to the Video Timeline. Canva updates often, so if a label looks a little different, look for the closest match.
Step 2 of 8

Upload your own media

  1. Open Uploads and select Upload files. Upload from your device: your VO, your tag, your sound bite clips and your game footage.
  2. Game clips are often long. Watch them once and write down the time of each play your script names, so you can find it fast in Step 4.
  3. Wait until every upload finishes before you start building.
Rubric · B-rollWide, medium and tight sequences. Steady, in focus, real action. A staged shot costs you a point. Stock video from Canva's library isn't your reporting, so don't use it.

Quick check: You missed the game-winning play on camera. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: VO, bites and tag

Build the package around the audio. The words decide the length, and the game footage goes on top.

  1. Drag your VO onto the timeline.
  2. Wherever your script calls for a bite, drag that sound bite clip onto the timeline.
  3. Put your tag at the very end. Your package ends on it.
  4. Trim a bite: select the clip, then drag its left or right handle. For exact cuts, zoom the timeline by holding Ctrl (Windows) or ⌘ (Mac) and scrolling.
  5. To split a clip, select it and put the playhead where you want the cut. Then press S, or use the ••• (More) menu → Split.
  6. To remove a section, split on both sides of it and delete the middle piece. Use this to cut gaps or flubs out of your VO.
Rubric · Sound biteOne complete thought, 8 to 12 seconds. A feeling or opinion. Clean out-cue.

Quick check: Which part of the player's interview makes the best bite?

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with game footage

  1. Under each line of VO, drag in the play that line describes. Show what the words say. If the VO names a player's serve, show that player serving.
  2. Split long game clips down to the action. Start just before the play and cut once it's over.
  3. Build sequences: a wide of the play, a medium of the player, a tight of the ball, hands or face.
  4. Use reaction shots (bench, crowd, coach) to show how the moment felt.
  5. Use straight cuts only. When you hover between two clips, Canva offers Add transition. Skip it, and don't add slow motion, animations or effects.
Rubric · EditPictures match words. Straight cuts. A mismatch or a repeated shot costs you a point.

Quick check: Your VO says, "Junior Jayden Brooks broke free for a 40-yard touchdown." What goes under that line?

Step 5 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Select a clip, then select the volume icon in the toolbar above the editor. Use the slider or type a number. Audio tracks and video clips both have it.
  2. Set your VO, bites and tag so the voices are clear and even. When you switch from VO to a bite, the loudness shouldn't jump.
  3. Turn the game footage's nat sound (crowd, whistles, the ball, the band) down so it plays under your VO. Never set it to zero. The rubric wants nat sound under every VO.
  4. Skip Balance all. It can push the crowd up to the same level as the voices.
  5. Bites recorded at the game may have crowd noise behind them. If the speaker is hard to hear, turn the bite up, or pick a cleaner bite.
  6. Play the whole package with headphones. Fix any spot that's too loud or too soft.
Rubric · AudioVoices clear at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound under every VO.
Canva's volume slider isn't a dB meter. Set levels by ear, then check the peaks the way your class checks audio before you turn in.

Quick check: The crowd is so loud it drowns out your VO. Best fix?

Step 6 of 8

Add the lower thirds

  1. Move the playhead to 1 second after a sound bite starts.
  2. Open Text, add a text box and type the speaker's name on one line. On the next line, type their title, like Head Volleyball Coach or Senior Outside Hitter.
  3. Drag it to the lower third of the frame, with nothing covering the speaker's face. Use a plain, readable style.
  4. On the timeline, drag the text layer's edges so it holds for 4 seconds. Text sits on its own track above the video. If you see page thumbnails instead of a timeline, switch to the Video Timeline.
  5. Repeat for every speaker. Then check each spelling against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · Lower thirdLower third correct: spelled right and timed right. In Cougar News, it starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.
Putting the final score or a stat on screen? Check it against the source one more time. Every name, stat and number is checked before the final.

Quick check: The online roster spells your player's name "Kaitlynn," but your notes say "Caitlin." What do you do?

Step 7 of 8

Check the run time and watch it all the way through

  1. Look at the package's total length on the timeline. It should be within 2 seconds of your target.
  2. If you're over, tighten VO gaps or trim plays down to the action. If you're under, ask your producer before you add anything.
  3. Look for any black frames or blank gaps between clips, especially at the very start and end.
  4. Watch it once all the way through, start to finish, with the rubric next to you. Make sure the package ends on your tag.
Rubric · TimingWithin 2 seconds of the target (:30 · :45 · :60). Read out loud and timed.

Quick check: Your target is :60 and your package runs :53. Where does that land in the Timing row?

Step 8 of 8

Download and turn it in

  1. Click Share in the top right, then Download.
  2. For file type, choose MP4 Video.
  3. For quality, choose 1080p. Skip 4K: the file is bigger and harder to share. Then click Download.
  4. Watch the downloaded file once to make sure the audio and lower thirds came through.
  5. Turn in everything the full turn-in asks for. A missing step costs you a point.
Rubric · Deadlines + respectFinal by Tuesday, end of class. Full turn-in. Nothing embarrasses anyone, including the other team.
Lost the download? Canva saves your design automatically. Open it again from Projects on the Canva home screen and download it again.
