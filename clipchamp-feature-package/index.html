Edit a Feature Package in Clipchamp

Turn your interviews, VO, b-roll and nat sound into a finished feature package, one step at a time.

About 9 minutes8 steps + final checkBuilt on the Feature Rubric
Start
Before you open Clipchamp

Have these ready

A feature package lives or dies on its people, pictures and sound. Don't open Clipchamp until you can check every box.

Use headphones for the whole edit. You can't hear room echo or a weak nat sound through laptop speakers.

Shortcuts you'll use: S split at the playhead · Space play/pause · Ctrl/Cmd + Z undo · Ctrl/Cmd + = / - zoom the timeline

Step 1 of 8

Start a blank project

  1. Open Clipchamp and select Create a new video. Skip the templates. Templates add title cards and effects. Your package should look like news.
  2. Click the project name at the top (it starts as Untitled video) and rename it with your slug, like FEATURE-robotics-team, so your producer can find it.
  3. Leave the frame at widescreen 16:9 to match the broadcast.
Clipchamp updates its layout often. If a button has moved, look nearby for the same name.
Step 2 of 8

Import, then pick your best shots

  1. Click Import media and add your VO, your interview clips and your b-roll. They show up in Your media.
  2. Wait until every file finishes importing before you start building.
  3. Watch your b-roll once. Set aside any shot that is shaky, blurry, too dark, too bright or the wrong color. Those cost points in Video Production, even if they're short.
  4. Find your best shot, the one with the strongest picture and nat sound. You'll open with it in Step 3.
Rubric · Video productionRule of thirds with head room and nose room. Every shot well lit, steady and in focus. Clear sequences, including WALLDO.

Quick check: Your only shot of the robot's first run is a little shaky. What should you do?

Step 3 of 8

Build the spine: open, VO and bites

Build the package around the audio. The words and nat sound set the story's shape, and the b-roll goes on top.

  1. Open with your best b-roll and let the nat sound pop, 1 to 3 seconds of real sound (a cheer, a buzzer, a laugh) before anyone talks.
  2. Drag your VO from Your media onto the timeline right after the open.
  3. No VO file? Select Record & create, then Audio. Allow the microphone, click the red mic button, read your script, then click Stop. Choose Retake recording if you stumbled, or Save and edit to keep it.
  4. Find each spot where a bite plays. Click the VO, park the playhead there and press S to split it. Drag the second half right to open a space.
  5. Drag the interview clip onto the video track in that space. Trim it: park the playhead where the thought starts, press S and delete the extra. Do the same where the thought ends. Use bites from at least 2 credible sources.
  6. Use the same split-and-delete move to cut gaps or flubs out of your VO.
  7. Check your gold coins: a strong bite or nat sound pop at the start, in the middle and at the end.
Rubric · Story structure + writingClear beginning, middle and end, with gold coins. The VO sounds like talking and doesn't repeat what the bites say.
Zoom in on the timeline (Ctrl/Cmd + =) to split exactly between words.

Quick check: Which open fits the Feature Rubric best?

Step 4 of 8

Cover the VO with b-roll

  1. Under each line of VO, drag in the b-roll that shows that line. Say dog, see dog. If the VO names a student soldering a wire, show that student soldering.
  2. Cut long clips down with S: keep the action and delete the rest.
  3. Build sequences: wide, then medium, then tight. Mix in WALLDO shots to keep it moving.
  4. Use each shot only once.
  5. Don't put two shots of the same person from the same angle back to back. That's a jump cut. Put a different shot between them.
  6. Use straight cuts only. Skip the Transitions tab, effects, filters and music.
Rubric · Visual storytellingStart with the best b-roll. Nat sound pops. Say dog, see dog. A shot used twice drops you to a 2.

Quick check: Your VO says, "Senior Maya Ortiz has rebuilt the arm three times." What goes under that line?

Step 5 of 8

Make J and L cuts

A J cut lets you hear the next clip before you see it. An L cut lets the sound keep going after the picture changes. Both make your edit flow, and the rubric needs them for a 4 in Sequences & Transitions.

  1. Select an interview clip on the timeline. Open the Audio tab in the property panel on the right and click Detach audio. The sound moves to its own row below the video, so you can move picture and sound separately.
  2. L cut: drag a b-roll clip onto the track above the last 2 or 3 seconds of the interview. The b-roll covers the speaker's face, but their voice keeps going underneath. We see what they're talking about.
  3. J cut: on the next interview, drag the left edge of the video (not the audio) 1 or 2 seconds to the right. Then stretch the b-roll before it to fill the space. We hear the speaker just before we see them.
  4. Play each cut back. The sound should flow under the picture change without a gap or a jump.
Rubric · Sequences & transitionsClean cuts. J and L cuts. No jump cuts or flash frames. With no J or L cuts, the best you can earn is a 3.
In Clipchamp, a clip on a higher track covers the one below it. That's what makes the L cut work: the b-roll sits on top, and the interview's sound keeps playing.

Quick check: You hear the marching band's drums for 2 seconds, then the picture cuts to the band. What kind of cut is that?

Step 6 of 8

Mix the audio

  1. Click a clip, then open the Audio tab in the property panel on the right.
  2. Set your VO and interviews so the voices are clear and even. When you switch from VO to a bite, the loudness shouldn't jump. The volume slider goes up to 200%.
  3. Drag the b-roll's volume down so its nat sound plays under your VO. Never drag it to 0, and don't detach and delete it.
  4. Between VO lines, bring the nat sound up for a moment so it pops: the buzzer, the laugh, the cheer. Split the b-roll clip with S so that piece can have its own volume.
  5. Interview has a hum or background buzz? Detach its audio, select the audio clip and switch Noise suppression to On. Listen before and after, and keep whichever sounds more natural.
  6. Play the whole package with headphones. Crackle or fuzz means a clip is too loud, so lower it.
Rubric · Audio productionVoices at −12 to −6 dB. Nat sound clear and in the right places. VO clear the whole way through.
Clipchamp's volume slider shows a percentage, not decibels. Set levels by ear in Clipchamp, then check the −12 to −6 dB peak the way your class does.

Quick check: Your VO is easy to hear, but you muted all the b-roll to get it that way. What happens on the rubric?

Step 7 of 8

Add lower thirds and fact graphics

  1. Open the Text tab in the left toolbar and choose a plain, readable style. Skip anything marked with a diamond. That's a premium feature, and it can block your export.
  2. Drag the text onto the timeline on a track above the interview.
  3. Start it 1 second after the interview starts. Drag its trim handles so it holds for 4 seconds.
  4. With the text selected, open the Text tab in the property panel. Type the speaker's name and title, like Robotics Team Captain or Engineering Teacher.
  5. In the preview, drag the text to the lower part of the frame, clear of the speaker's face.
  6. Repeat for every speaker. If a key fact needs to be on screen, like a date, place or number, add a graphic for it the same way. Then check each spelling and number against your confirmed notes, letter by letter.
Rubric · GraphicsCorrect graphics for every interview and any other facts. One misspelling drops you to a 2. In Cougar News, a lower third starts 1 second in and holds 4 seconds.

Quick check: The club's website spells the captain's name "Jaxon," but your notes say "Jackson." What do you do?

Step 8 of 8

Clean up, export and turn it in

  1. Look for gaps on the timeline. Hover over a gap and click Delete this gap, or right-click a tiny gap and choose it there. A gap shows up as a black flash frame.
  2. Watch the whole package once with the rubric next to you. Look for flash frames, jump cuts and repeated shots, especially at the start and end. Listen for every name: is each one said the way that person says it?
  3. Remove anything marked with a diamond (premium) so the export isn't blocked.
  4. Click Export in the top-right corner, choose 1080p and wait for the progress bar to finish. The MP4 saves to your computer, usually in Downloads.
  5. Watch the exported file once to make sure the audio and graphics came through.
  6. Turn in your draft on Friday. Fix what your producer flags, then turn in your final by Tuesday, end of class, with every turn-in step complete.
Rubric · Deadlines + respectDraft on Friday and final by Tuesday, end of class. Full turn-in. Nothing embarrasses anyone.
If Clipchamp asks about gaps when you click Export, choose to remove them. Lost the file? Open the project again from your Clipchamp home page and export it again.
